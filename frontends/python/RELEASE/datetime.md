RELEASE_TYPE: patch

This release moves date and time generation into the engine, which now bundles a pinned IANA time zone database, and adds `timezone_keys()` and `utc_offsets()` along with the date and time options every Hegel frontend shares.

`timezones()` and the new `timezone_keys()` draw IANA names from the engine's database, restricted to the names this Python's `zoneinfo` can load, so a saved failure means the same zone on every machine. Half the draws are weighted toward zones with a rare feature (a fractional offset, negative daylight saving, a skipped day), and a name shrinks down a ladder from `UTC` through the zones a reader recognises rather than alphabetically. Link names such as `Asia/Calcutta` are included and legacy names such as `EST5EDT` excluded by default; `links` and `legacy` switch each, and `names` restricts the draw to an explicit list.

`datetimes()` with a `timezones` generator now draws the zone and the wall-clock time in one engine draw when the zone is a `zoneinfo.ZoneInfo`: one draw in twenty on average lands within a second, an hour or a day of one of the zone's daylight-saving or offset transitions, or of a well-known instant such as the Unix epoch or the end of the signed 32-bit epoch. A repeated wall time is drawn with either value of `fold`, where `fold` used to be always 0, or with the one `ambiguous="earlier"` or `"later"` asks for, and a skipped wall time is reached deliberately; `allow_imaginary=False` now makes the engine draw a real wall time instead of rejecting the test case. `datetimes()` also accepts timezone-aware bounds, as Hypothesis does: both bounds are then moments in time, every datetime is aware and lies between them in a zone drawn from `timezones`, which defaults to `timezones()` and must not generate `None`. A bound may be omitted (`min_value` and `max_value` now default to `None`); one naive and one aware bound is an `InvalidArgument`.

`dates()` takes `weekdays` (numbered as `date.weekday()` numbers them) and `months` to draw only, say, weekends or Februaries, moving a drawn date to the nearest allowed one rather than filtering. `times()`, `datetimes()` and `timedeltas()` take a `precision`, one of the `timespec` names `isoformat` uses (`"milliseconds"`, `"seconds"`, `"minutes"` or `"hours"`), so every drawn time or delta is a whole number of that unit. The new `tzdb_version()` reports the IANA release of the engine's bundled database, for comparing with the host's `tzdata`. The new `utc_offsets()` draws fixed offsets as `datetime.timezone` objects, within bounds and at a `granularity` of hours, quarter-hours, minutes or seconds, targeting the offsets real zones have used; `from_type` resolves `datetime.timezone` to it and `datetime.tzinfo` and `zoneinfo.ZoneInfo` to `timezones()`.

`times()`, `datetimes()` and `timedeltas()` draw whole microseconds, Python's resolution, rather than nanoseconds truncated afterwards. `dates()` targets the leap days nearest the bounds, the year boundaries, the 2038 and 2106 overflow days and ISO week 53 in one draw of twenty on average, and `timedeltas()` round durations such as a second, an hour, a day or a week; a `timedelta` now shrinks toward zero as a whole, so the simplest negative delta is a microsecond below zero rather than a whole day.
